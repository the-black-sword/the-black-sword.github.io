The Scriptorium

From My Commonplace Book

Lines from other writers that I have copied out by hand and kept.

I check every attribution. The internet is full of quotations wearing borrowed names; where a line belongs to no one we can trace, it is credited honestly, with the name it usually travels under.

“Truth without kindness is cruel. Kindness without truth is manipulation.”

— Emma Watson

“Three things cannot long be hidden: the Sun, the Moon, and the Truth.”

— A popular saying, after the BuddhaThe canonical source, Aṅguttara Nikāya 3.129, says three things shine openly: the disk of the moon, the disk of the sun, and the teaching of the Buddha.

“Fear is the path to the dark side. Fear leads to anger. Anger leads to hate. Hate leads to suffering.”

— Yoda, Star Wars: Episode I – The Phantom Menace (1999)

“Resentment is like drinking poison and then hoping it will kill your enemies.”

— Anonymous (often attributed to Nelson Mandela)The saying is older than Mandela; it circulated in self-help and recovery writing decades before him.

“When you stop learning, you start dying.”

— Anonymous (often attributed to Albert Einstein)There is no record of Einstein saying it.

“Even an ordinary secretary or a housewife or a teenager can, within their own small ways, turn on a small light in a dark room.”

— Miep GiesOften shortened to “Be a small light in a dark room.”

“Paper has more patience than people.”

— Anne Frank, her diary, 20 June 1942

“Toute la sagesse humaine sera dans ces deux mots : Attendre et espérer.”

— Alexandre Dumas, The Count of Monte Cristo“All human wisdom is contained in these two words: Wait and hope.”
