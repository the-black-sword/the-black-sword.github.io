The Supernova Hunt

The Catalogues

Every galaxy in the hunt has several names. Each name comes from a catalogue, and each catalogue has a story.

Messier

Charles Messier was an eighteenth-century French comet hunter. To avoid mistaking fuzzy patches for comets, he listed the ones that never moved. His 110 “nuisances” became the best-loved list in astronomy, and many of the galaxies on our list carry his M numbers.

NGC and IC

The New General Catalogue, compiled by J. L. E. Dreyer in 1888, gathered nearly 8,000 nebulae and clusters, largely from the Herschels’ surveys. Two Index Catalogues (1895 and 1908) added thousands more.

Caldwell

In 1995 Sir Patrick Moore assembled 109 fine objects that Messier had missed, many in the southern sky, and named the list after his own surname, Caldwell-Moore.

Arp’s Atlas of Peculiar Galaxies

In 1966 Halton Arp published photographs of 338 galaxies that refused to look ordinary: galaxies colliding, trailing streams of stars, or torn by companions. Peculiar galaxies interest a supernova hunter because interactions can trigger bursts of star formation, and massive young stars are the ones that explode. Several of our galaxies are Arp objects:
