The Ancient World

Sumer, Egypt, Greece and Rome, in their own words and in the historians'.

Chosen from the shelf

11 books I would hand you first, and why.

  1. The Epic of Gilgamesh

    Anonymous

    Discovered in the ruins of the Library of Ashurbanipal at Nineveh, in today’s Iraq, this is one of the oldest surviving stories in human history, and many of its episodes echo through other tales of Mesopotamia and the Levant. Follow Gilgamesh, King of Uruk, as he meets friendship, loss, adventure, combat, the anger of the gods, and a quest for immortality.

  2. The Egyptian Book of the Dead: The Papyrus of Ani

    E. A. Wallis Budge

    The spells that Egyptians had copied onto papyrus and buried with them to guide them through the afterlife. This is the classic text for understanding the theology and mindset of the ancient Egyptians as they faced the same fate we all must: death.

  3. The Peloponnesian War

    Thucydides

    The best-known ancient account of the struggle between the two great powers of Greece, Athens and Sparta. Thucydides recounts the war between the world’s “first” democracy and the martial society of Sparta, between an unstoppable land power and a great sea power.

  4. Handbook to Life in Ancient Greece

    Lesley and Roy Adkins

    What was life actually like for an ordinary person in Athens, Thebes or Corinth? Here is how they lived, earned a living, ate, entertained themselves, and saw themselves within the Greek world.

  5. The Conquest of Gaul

    Julius Caesar (Penguin translation, revised by Jane Gardner)

    Julius Caesar in his own words. See how a Roman general wanted his age to see him, and how he used this book as propaganda for the conquest that would carry him to supreme power in Rome.

  6. Chronicle of the Roman Emperors

    Chris Scarre

    From Augustus, who set the standard for what an emperor would be, through the Five Good Emperors and the Crisis of the Third Century to the last days of Rome in the West: every emperor of Rome, however short his reign.

  7. Handbook to Life in Ancient Rome

    Lesley and Roy Adkins

    What was it like to live in ancient Rome? If you were rich, a time like no other. If you were poor, miserable and dangerous. And if you were a tradesman, noisy, smelly, full of entertainment, and the one place where the rest of the world might plausibly turn up on your doorstep. Here is the life of some typical Romans.

  8. Mythology: Timeless Tales of Gods and Heroes

    Edith Hamilton

    The classic collection of the Greek and Roman myths in one place, told in effortless prose. Whether you’re an old hand at the Greek myths or a raw beginner, the quest for the Golden Fleece, the Olympian gods and their appetites, and even the real cause of the Trojan War will keep you enthralled.

  9. Metamorphoses

    Ovid (Rolfe Humphries translation; Loeb edition, Books I–VIII and IX–XV)

    The great myths gathered by the poet Ovid into breathless tales of transformation, consequence, and gods meddling in human affairs: the classic text for how the Romans saw their gods and the gods’ dealings with humanity.

  10. The Sumerians: Their History, Culture, and Character

    Samuel Noah Kramer

    A portrait of the people of Sumer, who gave us the first cities, writing and law codes, by one of the great Sumerologists of the 20th century.

  11. Chronicle of the Pharaohs

    Peter A. Clayton

    Every ruler of ancient Egypt, reign by reign and dynasty by dynasty, from the first kings to the end of Ptolemaic Egypt, illustrated throughout.
